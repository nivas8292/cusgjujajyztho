🔤 Stretch phonics readers

Read Write Inc. Phonics (Oxford University Press)

33 titles in the borough 30 at Woolwich (Sets 1A–7) Sample copy out — due back 27 Sept
Why: This is the actual phonics scheme most English primary schools teach (including the schools you're visiting). Since Isha already knows her basic sounds, go straight for Sets 4–7 — these cover the harder digraphs and longer words she'll meet in late Reception and Year 1. Reading the same scheme at home as school uses is the single highest-value prep.

How to get: Browse the Woolwich shelves for Sets 4–7, or reserve a specific title free in the catalogue for Woolwich pickup.

Collins Big Cat Phonics for Letters and Sounds — Phase 4 & 5

12 titles in the borough 10 at Woolwich One checked copy on loan Also at Blackheath
Why: "Phase 4–5" is precisely where an advanced Reception child should be reading: Phase 4 = longer words with adjacent consonants (like tent, frog), Phase 5 = alternative spellings (like ai vs ay). These are the sounds Year 1 teaches in the spring term, so she'd arrive ahead.

How to get: On Woolwich shelves now; reserve free if the one you want is out.

Songbirds Phonics — Julia Donaldson (Oxford Reading Tree)

31 titles in the borough Woolwich stocks the series Level 4 sample at Eltham, out — due back 10 Oct
Why: Written by the Gruffalo author, so the stories are actually enjoyable (a big deal for keeping a 3–4 year old reading daily). The phonics progression mirrors school exactly — Levels 4–6 are the right stretch band for her. Great for building reading stamina because she'll want to finish them.

How to get: Reserve Levels 4–6 free in the catalogue for Woolwich pickup (e.g. Songbirds Phonics Level 4: Spike Says).

Usborne Phonics Readers

63 titles in the borough 54 at Woolwich Llamas in Pyjamas: 2 copies on the shelf now
Why: Rhyming, funny, fold-out surprises — these are "reading for pleasure" books that happen to be phonically decodable. Best used as the fun pile next to the more systematic Read Write Inc. books, so reading never feels like homework.

How to get: Walk in and grab — Woolwich is full of them.

📖 Confident readers — Year 1 level

Biff, Chip and Kipper — Oxford Reading Tree, Stages 4–6

182 titles in the borough 77 at Woolwich, incl. Stage 6 Checked copy available now
Why: The most widely used reading scheme in English schools — there's a good chance her Reception class will use these. Stage 5–6 is Year 1 territory (longer sentences, trickier words, simple chapters). Getting comfortable with the characters and format now means one less new thing in September 2027.

How to get: Plenty on Woolwich shelves now.

Ladybird Read It Yourself — Levels 2 & 3

41 titles in the borough 18 at Woolwich Level 3 sample available now
Why: Level 2 = "Developing Reader" (late Reception), Level 3 = "Confident Reader" (Year 1). The non-fiction titles (space, dinosaurs, animals) are brilliant for vocabulary — Year 1 comprehension depends heavily on background knowledge, not just decoding.

How to get: On Woolwich shelves now — mix one story + one non-fiction per visit.

🔢 Maths picture books

How Many Legs? — Kes Gray & Jim Field

Not at Woolwich In stock at Eltham, Blackheath, Greenwich Centre, West Greenwich Available now at Eltham & West Greenwich — free to reserve to Woolwich
Why: Counting in 2s, 4s and 10s disguised as a hilarious animal story — this is exactly the "counting in multiples" skill Year 1 teaches. Also builds the subitising habit (seeing quantities without counting one-by-one) that Reception assesses.

How to get: Reserve free in the catalogue → collect at Woolwich.

Not stocked — buy secondhand or try the free e-book apps

One Is a Snail, Ten Is a Crab The Doorbell Rang (Pat Hutchins) Anno's Counting Book Ten Black Dots (Donald Crews)
Why these matter: All four are classics for number bonds to 10, halving/doubling and early fractions — the exact Reception maths goals. The Doorbell Rang (sharing cookies among more and more children) is the single best picture book for division-as-sharing before Year 1.

Alternatives: Check them on the free Libby app first (below). Otherwise they're £2–4 secondhand on Vinted or World of Books — cheaper than the £1-per-item fee the library charges to order in a book it doesn't stock.

💳 Borrowing rules (Greenwich Libraries)

  • Borrowing books: free. Return to any Greenwich library.
  • Reserving a book that's in stock anywhere in the borough → free (transferred to Woolwich).
  • Ordering a book the library doesn't stock → £1 per item.
  • Overdue books: 20p/day. Under-15s get 5 free replacement cards.
  • Renew online in the catalogue or by phone: 01527 852 384.
  • Not published online: the loan period and max items per card — ask at the Woolwich desk.

📱 Free e-books & audiobooks (with your card)

  • Libby / OverDrive app — free e-books (21-day loans) and audiobooks (14-day loans), up to 5 at a time. Log in with your library card number + PIN. Great for holidays.
  • uLibrary app — audiobooks, up to 6 at a time for 21 days (choose "GLL-Greenwich"). Perfect for car rides.
  • Freegal — 3 music downloads/week + 3 hrs streaming/day (nursery rhymes galore).

Note: Greenwich uses OverDrive/Libby, not BorrowBox.
